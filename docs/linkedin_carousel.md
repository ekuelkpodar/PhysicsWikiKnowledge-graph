# LinkedIn carousel: 6 slides

Format 1080×1350, dark "archival observatory" style (ink #0b0f14, cream text, gold = laureates, dashed cyan = predicted). Screenshots come from the live app.

1. **Hook.** "A third of physics Nobel laureates descend from one man born in 1597." Visual: the radial Leibnütz tree with gold laureate nodes. Footer: *The Physics Family Tree: 11,931 physicists from Wikidata.*
2. **The dataset.** "11,931 physicists → 25,539 people once you follow advisor links. 18,454 advisor→student edges, 26 generations deep." Visual: the Graph view, force layout, coloured by field community.
3. **Who gets recognized.** "Laureate advisor: 29% won a major award. Everyone else: 9%. Adjusted HR 1.48 (1.27–1.73). Women: HR 0.56." Visual: the Kaplan–Meier chart (laureate advisor vs not) and the forest plot.
4. **Where they moved.** "Post‑1991: 76% of physicists from former Soviet republics took their jobs abroad (was 40%). 1933–45 Central Europe: 57% (was 45%)." Visual: the migration map at 1934 plus the era Sankey.
5. **What's missing.** "node2vec predicts missing advisor links: AUC 0.93. It ranks a hidden 'student of' teacher #1 in 34% of cases (chance: 3%)." Visual: Missing‑links cards with evidence chips.
6. **The data lesson + CTA.** "17% of 'physicists' aren't. 10 are fictional (Lex Luthor, Doc Brown, Gordon Freeman). Explore it, fix Wikidata, collaborate →" Visual: the Data quality view. Demo + repo links.
